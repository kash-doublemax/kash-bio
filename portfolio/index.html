01 · Product strategyMerkle Taiwan (dentsu Group) · Lead solution consultant

H2U Health Media Network: from ad inventory to a health-data product

H2U is a Taiwan-listed health media and membership company within Hon Hai (Foxconn) Group, the world's largest electronics manufacturer. Its brands cover health content, running, hiking, e-commerce and health checks.

~USD 3MAdvertising revenue target for 2027, the first commercial year
6 productsFrom managed media to audience packages, clean-room matching and data licensing
3 gatesLicensing, controlled delivery, standard packaging; each unlocks a new customer type

Situation

H2U had members, content and behavior data across several sites, but revenue still came from selling banner space. Data definitions differed across business, marketing and data teams, and there was no path to sell data on its own.

My role

Lead solution consultant. I consolidated the client's requirements, planned the system solution, and coordinated the technology and product teams that built the product and took it to market.

What I did

  • Designed the platform in three layers: identity and data governance, audience modeling, and activation with ad-performance feedback.
  • Wrote a stage-gated MVP plan with clear client and vendor responsibilities.
  • Authored the 2027–2028 product roadmap for the client's board: market sizing, six product tiers, phase gates, a five-metric board scorecard and a 90-day plan.
  • Built compliance into the product: de-identification, consent and licensing records that travel with the data, and gates mapped to Taiwan's personal-data and insurance rules.

Result

  • Data pipeline, audience platform and ad system are live; 25 standard audience packages are defined.
  • Roadmap presented to the board for the 2027 strategy cycle.
  • 2027 is the first commercial year, with an advertising revenue target of about USD 3 million.

02 · Go-to-marketADnalyst (funP / cacaFly group) · Product Director · 2014–2016

Launching Sociomantic, a global DSP, in Taiwan

Sociomantic (later part of dunnhumby) was one of the earliest real-time-bidding demand-side platforms, founded in Berlin in 2009. ADnalyst became its exclusive partner in Taiwan in March 2015.

Situation

Programmatic buying was new in Taiwan. Advertisers and agencies did not yet know what a DSP was, and most local options were limited in reach.

My role

Product Director, responsible for the go-to-market strategy: market education, agency channel, packaging and commercial partnership design.

What I did

  • Organized the public launch summit and ran roadshows with advertising agencies.
  • Packaged the technology into offers clients could buy, such as a retargeting solution for retail e-commerce: about 400 million daily impressions and over 95% reach of Taiwan's internet users, with a six-step onboarding process.
  • Designed the commercial partnership model with cacaFly's sales team as the co-selling channel.
  • Designed a publisher data-monetization model (2014) for premium magazine titles: publishers add a tracking tag, audience data is packaged for DSP buyers, and revenue is shared.

Result

  • Exclusive Taiwan distribution of Sociomantic announced in March 2015.
  • Earlier, sourced RTB products from the US, China, the UK and Germany and launched them in Taiwan.
  • The publisher data-monetization model is the same logic I applied at scale for H2U ten years later.
Verify

  • Client proposals: retargeting solution for a health-and-beauty retailer (2014); DMP partnership proposal for a magazine publisher (2014).
03 · Product developmentDoubleMax · Product Manager (2012) → Product Development Director (2013)

Building the ClickForce and MobiForce ad networks

DoubleMax was set up in 2012 as a joint venture between cacaFly and ClickForce, one of Taiwan's largest ad networks, to build and run an automated ad-delivery platform. It started as two desks in a shared office. I owned the product from specification to launch and worked with clients to roll it out.

500+Independent publisher sites on the platform
~USD 300KAnnual net profit, reached within about two years
3 engineersThe team I led to build and run two platforms: web, and mobile plus Facebook social

What I built

  • Wrote the product specifications and user manuals for both platforms.
  • Self-service back office for advertisers and publishers: campaigns, placements, reporting, billing and reconciliation.
  • CPM and CPC pricing, with an eCPM-based delivery logic that balanced advertiser performance and publisher revenue.
  • A permission model built on "operating scope plus function groups", so one person could act in several roles without multiple accounts.
  • Facebook fan-page post ads with a page-owner approval workflow, and mobile landing pages.
  • Retargeting and interest-based targeting products.

Result

  • By 2014 ClickForce reached about 900 partner sites (88% coverage) and MobiForce about 450 mobile media partners.
  • In October 2014 a wholly owned subsidiary of Chunghwa Telecom, Taiwan's largest telecom operator, announced an equity investment in ClickForce.

What it taught me

A platform serves several customers at once. Each role (advertiser, agency, publisher, publisher rep) needs its own view, permissions and money flow, and the product only works when all of them do.

How I work

Three habits from these cases

Start from who pays

Every roadmap begins with the buyer, the offer and the price logic, then works back to the platform.

Gate by evidence

Each phase has a pass condition, such as a first paying client or a repeatable package, before the next one is funded.

Design compliance in

Consent, licensing and data rules are product features, especially in health, insurance and finance.
