01 · P&LadHub · Business Development Director → Vice President (2018) · 2016–2022

adHub: from an internal concept to over USD 6M annual revenue

adHub was a new business inside the cacaFly group that became an Official Facebook Ad Reseller. I was responsible for its revenue, cost and profit.

USD 6M+Annual revenue reached within three years (over NTD 200M)
Full P&LRevenue, cost and profit
13 people · 3 headsOperations, sales and planning, each with its own manager

Situation

The group saw an opening in self-serve social advertising and needed someone who understood technology, sales and operations to turn the idea into a profitable stand-alone business.

My role

Business owner: market entry, annual business planning, revenue forecasting, resource allocation and team building. Promoted to Vice President in 2018.

What I did

  • Split the team into operations, sales and planning, and developed a manager for each, so I could focus on direction, P&L and key accounts.
  • Built one operating model from media planning and cross-channel operations to creative optimization and reporting, so service quality did not depend on individuals.
  • Ran regular revenue forecasts, account plans and performance reports as the basis for resource and commercial decisions.
  • Built in-house tools for efficiency and differentiation: adHub Trader Dashboard (on Microsoft Power BI) and ACHO, a LINE customer-data tool (2018).

Result

  • Over NTD 200 million (about USD 6 million) annual revenue within three years.
  • Clients included Philips, Lenovo, Taobao, Tmall, EVA Air and 30+ apparel e-commerce brands.
  • 2018 Digital Singularity Award, Programmatic Advertising (Lenovo team).
  • Per a 2021 profile, adHub had served over 2,000 clients and delivered about USD 100 million in ad spend.
02 · New venturesfunP / cacaFly group · 2012–2016

Three times handed a new business to build from zero

Each time the group saw a new opportunity, it gave me a new business to build and run independently. adHub was the third; these were the first two.

DoubleMax (2012–2013)

  • A cacaFly–ClickForce joint venture that started as two desks in a shared office.
  • Product Manager, promoted to Product Development Director in 2013; led a three-person engineering team to build web and mobile-social ad networks.
  • 500+ publisher sites and about USD 300K annual net profit within about two years.

ADnalyst (2014–2016)

  • Product Director, bringing real-time-bidding advertising to Taiwan.
  • Secured exclusive Taiwan distribution of Sociomantic in 2015; ran the launch summit and agency roadshows, and designed co-selling with the group's sales team.
  • Sourced products from the US, China, the UK and Germany and launched them in Taiwan.

What these taught me

A new business needs real authority and backing from the owner. In return, the operator's job is to build it to the point where systems and a team can carry it. That is how I see a founder transition: what is handed over is not a seat, but a mechanism that runs by itself.

03 · TransformationdentsuX Taiwan · Vice President & Chief Transformation Officer · 2021–2023

dentsuX: changing how an established agency operates

Recruited by the managing director in August 2021, reporting to the COO, leading a business unit and the company's organizational transformation.

Situation

Insights, media, content, MarTech and e-commerce ran separately, while clients wanted integrated service and the market demanded faster response.

What I did

  • Integrated separate capabilities into one client-service model across the customer journey.
  • Introduced an Agile operating framework so planning, media, social and data science teams could re-prioritize quickly.
  • Modernized services and operations, moving cross-department work through influence rather than authority.

Result

  • Associated with a projected 30%+ sales uplift in 2022 (projection).
  • Managed multinational clients including KFC, Warner Bros., Bausch + Lomb, Activision Blizzard and Coach.

How I lead

ICF Professional Certified Coach and Certified Scrum Master. I use coaching to build managers' judgment, and clear cadence and rules so teams can run on their own.

If I took the COO role

How I would approach the first 100 days

Days 1–30 · Listen

One-to-ones with the founder and every manager; understand the P&L, the key metrics and where decisions are actually made.

Days 31–60 · Decision rights

Map the organization and decision rights; set a weekly, monthly and quarterly operating cadence so decisions have a fixed place to happen.

Days 61–90 · Team

Agree annual targets and budgets with managers, identify successors for key roles and start coaching the management team.

After 90 days · Hand-over

Agree with the founder which decisions move to the management team and which stay at the strategy level, reviewed each quarter.

This is my working framework; the actual steps would follow what I learn about the company.
